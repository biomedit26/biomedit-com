Every drug we take is a losing race against our own clearance machinery. The body degrades the molecule, flushes it, and the level falls below the point where it does anything. So we dose again. Daily pills, weekly injections, monthly infusions. The schedule is not a feature of the disease. It is a feature of the fact that the drug cannot stay.

The alternative has a name and a long history of not working. Put a living cell in the body, have it take up residence, and let it make the drug on site, continuously, for as long as you need it. The microbiome is the obvious place to try it, because the gut already hosts trillions of bacteria that live there permanently and would be delighted to keep doing so.

On Sept 29, USDA's Center for Veterinary Biologics conditionally licensed a product that does exactly that. It's a strain of Lactobacillus reuteri engineered to secrete antibodies, and it was approved for chickens.
